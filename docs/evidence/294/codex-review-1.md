- **[P1] The PWA clock can overflow at enlarged text sizes.** [RunClock.tsx:105](/Users/richie/code/quickr-worktrees/fix-294-clocks/frontend/src/components/runs/RunClock.tsx:105) uses rem-based minimum font sizes without a width constraint or scaling fallback. With a 32 px root font size, the short-duration minimum is 120 px; a five-character time such as `59:59` is roughly 360 px wide in the monospaced font, before card padding. It cannot fit a 320–390 px viewport and may overflow or wrap. The hour-format minimum has the same problem. The clamp-class tests do not render or measure this case.

- **[P2] The iOS segment clock can become much smaller than 60% of the total.** [RunViews.swift:79](/Users/richie/code/quickr-worktrees/fix-294-clocks/ios/App/Runs/RunViews.swift:79) keeps the label beside the segment clock at every non-accessibility Dynamic Type size. On narrow screens or with a long label, that leaves less width for the segment than for the total; `RunDuration` can independently shrink it to 50% of its nominal size. The nominal 60/96 font ratio therefore does not ensure the rendered ratio, particularly for `h:mm:ss`. A width-aware stacked layout would give the clock more room.

- **[P2] The PWA repeats the segment label for screen readers.** [RunPanel.tsx:168](/Users/richie/code/quickr-worktrees/fix-294-clocks/frontend/src/components/runs/RunPanel.tsx:168) exposes the visible `head.text`, then passes the same text as `Duration`’s screen-reader-only label. A screen reader encounters “Rest 1, Rest 1, …” rather than one label and its time.

The test exercises only a freshly started duration, while [RunClockSize.test.tsx:28](/Users/richie/code/quickr-worktrees/fix-294-clocks/frontend/src/components/runs/RunClockSize.test.tsx:28) compares declared clamp bounds rather than rendered widths.
